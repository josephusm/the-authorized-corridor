# A Third Category Between Floating Signifier and Carrier

So far the corridor has worked on what it sees, what it preserves, what it signs. But there is a lower plane, less administrative and more poisonous: the words through which the corridor makes everything else thinkable.

Not words as ornament. Not the language of brochures, though that does damage with the reliable persistence of a leaking pipe. I mean the categories that arrive before the argument. The terms already laid out on the table when you try to describe a crisis: security, transparency, accountability, abuse, innovation, resilience, capacity building, humanitarian intervention, consent, risk. They look like tools. Often they are tools. The problem begins when the tool no longer measures the object it claims to measure, yet keeps passing every inspection because once it really did measure it.

This is the third category.

Caronia, following the line of the floating signifier, gives a name to a kind of sign that marks the inadequacy of the symbolic system. The floating signifier does not pretend to close the gap between word and world. It marks it. In practice, it says: our vocabulary is not enough here, but we still have to handle something. Its value lies partly in this honesty. It is not a trick of precision. It is a warning light on the dashboard.

The viral carrier stands on the other side. It does not mark the gap: it crosses it. It does not need semantic coherence because it travels on affect, bandwidth, urgency, recognizability. Kony 2012, Ice Bucket, Bring Back Our Girls: the specific payload may wear out, drift, be disproved, become almost secondary. The carrier keeps moving as long as it has infrastructure to run on. Debunking barely scratches it because the truth of the sentence was never its engine. Its engine was the sentence's ability to become a vehicle.

Floating signifier and carrier both occupy the unstable point between signifier and signified, but from opposite sides. The floating signifier admits the void. The carrier monetizes it, accelerates it, uses it as an express lane. One says: I cannot name this object without leaving a residue. The other says: it does not matter; move.

The inherited signifier is neither.

It is not a floating signifier, because it does not originate as a sign of inadequacy. At first it had a reasonably solid referent. It was not empty. It was not a placeholder. It was not mana, not pure ideological foam. When someone spoke of “humanitarian intervention” at a certain historical moment, or “capacity building” within certain geopolitical arrangements, or “parliament” within a political form still crossed by real conflict, they were not necessarily manipulating a semantic carcass. The term could point to an existing structure—partial, dirty, contestable, but real.

Nor is it a carrier, because it does not simply bypass meaning. On the contrary: it protects itself through the memory of a meaning. Its strength does not come from content being irrelevant. It comes from content having genuinely mattered once. Old accuracy becomes its armor.

The inherited signifier is a classification accurate at time T, whose referent changes at time T+n while the label and the audits remain those of time T. The row is not updated. Not because everyone is stupid, though the world is always generous with evidence in that direction. Not because nobody has access to the dictionary. The point is more serious: the system does not know it needs to build a new check, because the old check keeps passing.

That is what makes it dangerous. A carrier, once exposed, at least remains suspect. A floating signifier, if you listen to it, warns you that the vocabulary is giving way. The inherited signifier, instead, produces calm. It has documents. It has history. It has founding cases. It has legitimate citations. It has procedures built when the word still worked. If you challenge it, you are the one who appears not to understand continuity. The word presents its birth certificate and demands that it be accepted as proof of life.

But the world has changed beneath the word.

Take “capacity building.” In some contexts it could mean the actual construction of skills, administrations, local infrastructure, margins of autonomy. Then the balance of power changes, the actors change, dependencies change, money flows change, the political referent changes. The term remains. The reports remain. The metrics remain. The database column remains. The old audit asked: does this activity increase capacity according to the model defined then? The question now required is different: what dependency is it reproducing while calling it capacity? But that question is not on the form.

The same mechanism applies to “humanitarian intervention.” A formula can arise within a regime where at least part of the referent is legible: protection, crisis, exposed populations, international responsibility, moral obligation. Then the referent is colonized by regime-change strategies, military outsourcing, geopolitical selectivity, the media management of suffering. The formula retains its ability to evoke the original moment. Every time you criticize it, someone can point to the case in which it seemed to mean what it said. The precise use of the past authorizes the vague use of the present.

This is the equivocation that keeps the inherited signifier alive.

Ordinary equivocation is synchronic: a word slides between two meanings within the same argument. Here the drift is diachronic: the word stays almost still while the world moves. But when you use it today, diachronic drift manifests as synchronic ambiguity. You say “security” and move, without declaring it, between the concrete protection of bodies, service continuity, institutional reputation, insurance compliance, reduction of legal risk, control of other people's behavior. You say “transparency” and oscillate between access to the system's seam and a prefabricated explanation panel. You say “consent” and pass from situated desire to a checkmark produced by a hostile interface. The word appears singular. The referents are multiple. The old referent lends respectability to the new one.

The corridor loves this form because it is cheap. It does not have to invent a language of capture from scratch. It only has to inherit a language that has already passed the field's moral defenses. A word born to open can be reused to close at lower cost. It already has friends. It already has a literature. It already has courts, policies, courses, forms, experts, committees. Capture does not present itself as capture. It presents itself as continuity.

This is where the corridor works as a reducer of overflow. In the contemporary digital and institutional environment there is too much signification: too many signals, too many cases, too many identities, too many hybrid harms, too many overlapping scales, too many near-events that will not fit into a single column. A crude corridor would cut all of it away. A more mature corridor does something smarter: it freezes a set of authorized words and forces the overflow to pass through them. It does not merely produce slots. It produces uncritical inheritance.

The result is a perceptual pacification. The world exceeds, but the vocabulary remains stable. Situations change, but dashboards can keep using the same category families. Institutions can say they are updating procedures while retaining the names that prevent them from seeing what truly needs to be updated. Verification then fails to detect the drift because it looks for defects inside the term, not in the historical relation between term and referent. It checks the label, not the slow death of the object.

The dirtiest part is that often nobody is lying in the simple sense.

The first generation that builds a delegation for deniability knows it is staging theater. It knows that a formula serves to keep a margin open, distribute responsibility, permit a maneuver that cannot be stated in full. Deniability is performative: every adult in the room understands the game, even while pretending otherwise. Then the people change. The documents remain. The procedures remain. The formulas remain. The second generation inherits the theater without the subtitles. What was performance becomes epistemology.

This passage is brief, and therefore almost invisible. For a while, those who knew coexist with those who do not yet know. That is the moment when the device could be seen. Then staff rotate, memos become archives, exceptions become policy, context thins out. The new generation no longer pretends to believe. It believes in the perimeter it received. Not because it is more naive, but because the meta-knowledge was not transmitted. The theater has become the world.

Here the inherited signifier ceases to be merely an old word. It becomes an environment.

The social contract can work this way. Parents know that a certain promise is an agreement: study, work, obey, and in return you will receive mobility, security, recognition. They do not mistake it for natural law; they live it as a historical contract, fragile, perhaps unjust, but legible. Their children receive the same formula after the material referent has changed. Work no longer guarantees the same trajectory, obedience no longer buys the same future, sacrifice no longer pays back in the same currency. But the formula remains hanging there as though it were still ontology. When it empties out, the response does not immediately look like revolt. It may look like lying flat: I no longer believe your word, but I do not yet have another grammar through which to leave.

This is the bridge toward illegible resistance, but we are not there yet. First we must see the capture.

The inherited signifier captures critique before it can be formulated because it decides in advance which terms are available for criticizing it. If you say that a “security” procedure produces insecurity, you have to use the word security inside a field the device already controls. If you say that a “transparency” policy produces opacity, you risk being referred to the volume of documents published. If you say that “consent” was not consent, you will be shown the checkmark. Critique always arrives one step late, forced to explain why the authorized word no longer means what the authorized word has the historical right to mean.

It is a losing position by design. Not impossible. Losing. Because you have to prove the drift, while the corridor only has to show formal continuity. You have to reconstruct the history of the referent; it only has to display the genealogy of the term. You have to bring cases, conditions, fractures, material transformations. It brings the name. And the name arrives first.

Parrhesia breaks this economy when it refuses to be a placeholder. Truthful speech does not merely say something uncomfortable; it insists on an anchored referent against a form that would prefer moldable content. This is why the corridor treats it as noise. Not because it fails to understand the words, but because it understands the danger too well: a word re-anchored to the world forces the system to stop living on the semantic credit of the past.

Semantic credit is the inherited signifier's hidden capital.

An institution can spend for years the respectability accumulated when a term still worked. It can keep saying participation after reducing participation to ornamental consultation. It can say oversight after outsourcing the very cost that produced involuntary oversight. It can say accountability after turning the signature into a terminal of liability. It can say security after replacing protection with reputational risk management. Each word carries a small historical rent. The corridor collects that rent and calls it stability.

The question, then, is no longer only: is this category true or false? Too easy, and often too late. The better question is: what version of the world must still be alive for this word to remain honest? If that version is dead, who benefits from the fact that its death certificate has not been filed? Which audit keeps measuring the wrong body? Which generation knew it was theater, and which inherited it as reality?

The inherited signifier is the point where the corridor stops filtering only objects and begins filtering the conditions of critique. It does not necessarily stop you from speaking. It hands you words already mortgaged. It leaves you free to argue within a vocabulary that has already decided where the load-bearing walls stand.

That is why the third category matters. The floating signifier marks the gap. The carrier exploits it. The inherited signifier buries it beneath an old correctness. It does not say: there is no referent. It says: the referent was there, so stop asking where it went.

The next step is to follow this burial all the way down. Because a critique that arrives after the authorized word must first free itself from the word itself. And often, by the time it finally begins to speak, the corridor has already written the vocabulary of its defeat.
