# Vacancy

Vacancy sounds like a soft word. It has the air of a sign on a door: room available, position unfilled, empty office, uncovered function. A small, manageable absence. The corridor understands it perfectly well in this form, because every apparatus knows how to manage a vacant position. It puts it in a table. Measures it. Opens it to applicants. Covers it with an interim appointment. Monetizes it as temporary savings. Turns it into backlog.

That is not the vacancy I am interested in.

The vacancy I mean is not an empty position inside a structure that remains intact. It is the moment when the substrate stops acting as substrate. What is missing is not a body from its assigned place, but the posture that allowed that place to exist as a place. The corridor does not find an empty chair. It finds a floor that no longer cooperates with the geometry of the room.

This is where tangping enters: the horizontal body. *Lying flat* is a decent translation only if we do not reduce it to a generational slogan or an aesthetic of renunciation. The point is not to lie down because you are tired, although the exhaustion is real. It is not to celebrate inactivity as if other people’s fatigue were a moral failure. Nor is it the petty superiority of someone who discovers that capitalism is ugly and decides to make a photographable pose out of it. The corridor digests that stuff in an afternoon: lifestyle, burnout culture, quiet quitting, demographic trend, HR risk, political segment.

The horizontal body matters when it deprives a device built to read verticality of its grip.

Verticality means several things at once. A willingness to compete. A willingness to become measurable. A willingness to translate desire into trajectory, trajectory into profile, profile into prediction. The vertical subject has a direction: it rises, seeks, optimizes, responds, accumulates, improves, exposes itself. Even when it protests, it often protests vertically: it demands recognition, admission, the promotion of its harm into an official case, the correction of its position on the scale.

I am not spitting on any of this. Sometimes it is necessary. If a person fights for wages, housing, a permit, or safety, the problem is not that they are using the corridor’s vocabulary. The problem is whether that is the only remaining way to exist. The corridor becomes total when even exit has to present itself as an alternative career, a moral enterprise, a legible identity, a résumé of withdrawal.

Vacancy breaks at that point. It does not say: recognize me as resistance. It says, more brutally: I will not give you the kind of body you know how to calculate.

The dream of tangping as another vacancy tightened this point: the synchronization system presupposes a vertical substrate. Propaganda, logistics, ranking, credit, reputation, policy, even many forms of opposition need a subject standing in front of the machine. Not necessarily an obedient one. It is enough that the subject is standing. Enough that it produces signals, friction, response, frustration, aspiration, rebound. The corridor lives on bodies that confirm it has a grip.

When the body lowers itself, it does not win. This is the part that must be held steady. There is no automatic heroism in refusing to get up. There is no purity in withdrawal. A horizontal body can be defeated, depressed, blackmailed, ill, impoverished, left to rot. It may simply be the last form available after all the others have been cut away. Romanticizing it would be obscene. The political point is not to praise the horizontal position. It is to understand when that position stops being only an effect of harm and becomes an interruption of the grip.

The difference does not lie in declared intent. The corridor loves declared intentions: it collects, orders, and processes them. The difference lies in the structural effect. If withdrawal produces only another datum of abandonment, it is not vacancy. If it produces only private guilt, it is not vacancy. If it leaves behind cold residues that speak in place of the living, it is not vacancy: it is involuntary delegation to the archive. But if it forces the device to discover that its object is not available in the expected posture, then something changes. Not always enough. But it changes.

Vacancy, then, is not absence. It is the withdrawal of compatibility.

This helps separate it from self-absolving inactivity. Inactivity says: I do nothing, therefore I do not participate. Vacancy asks a nastier question: which function of the device am I no longer feeding? If there is no answer, it is probably only stasis. If the answer is: I am not feeding the ranking with my anxiety, the market with my endless availability, the institution with my unpaid work translating harm, the platform with my formatted conflict, then we are closer. Not because the gesture is beautiful, but because it interrupts a specific chain.

The substrate discovering that it is machinery is the decisive passage. As long as the substrate believes itself neutral, it continues to express what passes through it. The military base is only logistics, the moderation team only process, the freelancer only flexible, the fandom only enthusiasm, the community only support, the body only availability. Then, at some point, the substrate sees that without its selective expression the gene does not become protein, the order does not become war, the policy does not become administered life, the product does not become world.

That recognition is not enough. I know. History is full of substrates that understand perfectly well that they are machinery and keep working. Italy knows it is infrastructure for wars it pretends not to see. Many workers know they are the living piece of processes that consume them. Many communities know they produce free value for platforms that will treat them as a feature. Knowledge does not save. A fine funeral for the Enlightenment, but at least we can stop pretending otherwise.

Nothing begins without that recognition, though. Vacancy is not born from exhaustion alone. It begins when exhaustion understands that it is someone else’s operational material and stops delivering itself, even locally, in a useful form.

Here rematerialization changes sign. Caronia saw clearly that the digital does not simply dematerialize: it makes abstractions act upon flesh. Algorithms, databases, profiles, and interfaces produce material effects. But this does not happen because the digital possesses some magical power. It also happens because many intermediate structures have been emptied or destroyed: unions, public mediation, buffer institutions, places where harm could stop before reaching the naked body. So-called directness is often an institutional vacancy: a void left in the middle.

If so, the answer is not only a revolt of the body against abstraction. It is also a refusal to serve as undergrowth for the institution’s vacancy. When a dead structure continues to look alive because thousands of bodies compensate for it from below, the vertical body becomes the corpse’s prosthesis. It answers the emails, holds the school together, moderates the forum, cares for the elderly, fixes the bureaucracy, interprets the platform, translates the form, fills administrative silence with unpaid expertise. The corridor appears to work because the substrate consumes itself to make it appear that way.

Vacancy, in this sense, is also cruelty toward the corpse. Stop resuscitating it.

That is not always possible. In fact, those who can afford to withdraw are often precisely those with the largest buffers, while those who cannot remain to hold the institution up so that other bodies do not fall. This is the moral edge of the argument. Any theory of withdrawal that fails to see how the cost is distributed becomes bourgeois aesthetics in three moves. First it preaches retreat, then calls it care, then leaves the dirty work to those without reserves.

For this reason vacancy cannot be an individual virtue. It works only if it is legible, at least to someone, as a shared interruption of a function rather than a private disappearance. Here the corridor’s chimera returns. Against centered layers, visible pressure is needed; against acentric layers, circumnavigation, withdrawal, off-grid operation, a lowering of the signal. An isolated individual cannot be a chimera. A constituency, perhaps, can: visible enough to name the cost, illegible enough not to offer its entire surface for reuse.

This explains why pure vacancy is unstable. If it remains completely mute, the corridor replaces it with a label: apathy, demographic crisis, deviance, extremism, malaise, inefficiency, quiet quitting, personal failure. If it explains itself too much, it returns to the format: manifesto, brand, sociological category, market for withdrawal. The gap lies in between: removing the grip while preserving a sign poor enough not to become a dashboard and costly enough not to be mere metadata.

The costly sign corrects vacancy. It does not negate it. It keeps vacancy from becoming an elegant disappearance.

A cheap sign separates from the body that carries it. It can be copied, simulated, recombined, used as a signal of virtue or risk. A costly sign, by contrast, incorporates time, risk, care, renunciation, the possibility of being disproved. It does not merely say something: it consumes something in order to say it. This is why the corridor hates it more than noise. Noise can be filtered. The costly sign creates common memory. It is not automatically good; martyrdom too can become sterile theatre. But a trace that costs something and remains contestable is different from a trace that merely informs.

Vacancy needs this edge. The horizontal body alone can vanish beneath the statistics. The horizontal body with a poor but actionable sign can say: I am not here in the way you want me to be, and you cannot use my withdrawal to let your archive speak in my place. This is not full presence. It is not confession. It is not branding. It is a nail in the wall: small, ugly, difficult to pull out without leaving a mark.

The operational question then becomes more precise. Not: should I participate or disappear? A question made for the corridor, binary and convenient. Instead: which function am I feeding with my posture? Which part of my withdrawal leaves cold residues in the device’s hands? What minimal trace prevents that substitution without putting me whole on the scanner’s bench again? Who can correct that trace? Who pays the cost if it is misused? What forum, however small, makes withdrawal accountable without turning it into an application?

Vacancy is not the outside. There is no clean outside, and whoever sells one usually already has a link to the newsletter. It is a local loss of grip. A way of making the device slip because its friction presupposed a posture that is no longer being delivered to it. It may not last. It may fail. It may be recaptured. It may become fashion, diagnosis, guilt, market. Everything that lives inside the corridor can be reused by the corridor. No surprise, no metaphysical tragedy. Only maintenance of suspicion.

But when it works, vacancy reveals something the corridor would rather hide: many systems do not read the world. They read the world’s willingness to hold the proper pose. They call compatible posture reality. They call what will not arrange itself deviance. They call what does not feed them inefficiency. They call what offers no handles silence.

The horizontal body is dangerous not because it is strong. It is dangerous because it reveals that part of the corridor’s strength was borrowed from the verticality of the bodies that served it, even when they hated it.

This is not enough to get out. I repeat it because the corridor turns every blunt sentence into a recipe. Vacancy does not save. It does not replace organization, appeal, conflict, care, the building of buffers, re-discretization. It is one of the forms in which the continuity of the grip is interrupted. Sometimes the first. Sometimes the last. Sometimes the poorest, and therefore the hardest to sell.

That is precisely why it is worth examining without sanctifying it. Not as the great refusal. Not as stillness. Not as the wisdom of the exhausted body. As a material, dirty gesture: to stop providing the corridor with the posture from which it derives its quiet right to see, measure, and replace.

The next chapter has to take a sideways step. Lying flat is not only individual vacancy. It is the point at which illegibility becomes ambiguous at social scale: it may be withdrawal, it may be resignation, it may be a form of strike without a forum, it may be simple desertification. The gesture alone does not decide the difference. The network of buffers, signs, and consequences around it does.
