# Śūnyatā as Involuntary Capture

Śūnyatā enters this book as a suspicious ally.

At first it looked like the right knife. If the authorized corridor lives by treating categories as substances, then anti-substantialism should wound it. If the carrier works because it circulates a sign as though it had a stable referent, then saying that referent has no intrinsic nature should pull the floor out from under the carrier. If Hormuz is a relation rather than a thing, if renewal is a practice rather than a fate, if a signature is a seam rather than an identity, then śūnyatā—emptiness of autonomous existence, no svabhāva, no intrinsic nature[^sunyata-nagarjuna]—looks like the perfect solvent.

Nice theory. Pity about the world.

The point is not that the diagnosis is false. If anything, it is almost too true. Hormuz has no intrinsic nature. It is a throat of water made strategic by flows, contracts, energy infrastructure, navies, insurance, sanctions, futures prices, maps, and fear. Change the oil, the pipelines, the demand, the military capacity, and Hormuz changes. Its substance is a hardened relation. And yet the world treats it as a strategic substance. It names Hormuz as though it were a fixed switch. The chokepoint becomes geopolitical svabhāva.

That is the first piece of bad news: knowing that a category is empty does not stop the category from operating as full.

The second is worse. In an asymmetrical field, dissolving categories universally can help the corridor more than those trying to resist it. The corridor is not a naive substantialist. It is not sitting in a metaphysical temple, sincerely believing that its classifications possess intrinsic nature. It works in a cruder and more effective way: it dissolves other people’s handles and builds handles on them. It turns objects into services, events into gradients, collectives into profiles, use value into a price feed, presence into telemetry. It removes discreteness where others might get a grip; it produces discreteness where it needs to grasp.

Śūnyatā, taken as a formal operation and isolated from its ethics, dissolves everything.

And that is where the problem begins.

In a symmetrical field, dissolution can be liberation. Two interlocutors who set aside inherited substances together can reopen their relation. A community that stops believing in the fixed nature of a category can correct the way it uses that category. A living forum can say: this name is not a thing, it is a practice; let us look at whom it harms, whom it protects, whom it authorizes. There śūnyatā is mental hygiene. It removes idolatry. It lets the relation breathe.

But the authorized corridor is not a symmetrical field. We are not sitting around a table with an equal right to dissolve and rebuild. On one side are those who can redesign formats, metrics, interfaces, policies, rankings, access, expiration dates, thresholds of proof. On the other are those who must remain recognizable within those formats long enough not to lose work, housing, voice, reputation, passage, care, credit, petty citizenship. If all you say in that field is “categories are constructed,” you have not yet done politics. Often you have done stage maintenance.

Power loves being told that everything is fluid when it owns the pumps.

This is not an argument against Nāgārjuna, Buddhism, deconstruction, or anti-essentialism. That would be a stupid shortcut, and the book already has enough easy targets without inventing more. The point concerns a single operation: dissolving handles without operational reconstruction. A living tradition is not exhausted by that operation; it carries discipline, ethics, practice, community, exercise. But when the dissolving gesture is extracted as a critical style and moved into a field governed by platforms, bureaucracies, markets, and proprietary interfaces, it can become involuntary capture.

Not because it is too radical. Because it is too polite toward those who have already learned how to show no visible substance.

The contemporary corridor does not present itself as essence. It presents itself as an update, process, safety, experience, flow, personalization, compliance, ecosystem. It has read enough theory not to be caught holding an idol. Tell it identity is relational and it nods. Tell it the subject is distributed and it smiles. Tell it the category is unstable and it opens a menu. Its strength does not lie in defending eternal categories. It lies in deciding who may change category, when, with what trace, before what forum, and at what cost.

Here diagnostic śūnyatā sees the trick, but operational śūnyatā risks removing the very thing needed to strike it: a handle.

Handle is an undignified word, which makes it suitable. A point of purchase. A local object instead of an evaporated service. An event instead of a gradient. A collective instead of a sum of profiles. A diff instead of an “evolving system.” An attributed line instead of a warm voice. A right of rollback instead of an explanation. This is not essence. It is constructed discreteness. It is the opposite of naive substantialism and the opposite of fluidity surrendered to the owner of the infrastructure.

Re-discretization does not say: this category is true by nature.

It says: this category is a lever, built here, for this purpose, within these limits, and if you touch it you must leave a trace.

It may sound like a subtle difference. It is not. It is the difference between an identity that becomes a prison and a category that becomes a tool. Between a flag used to pin someone down and a flag used to gather enough bodies to negotiate. Between a diagnosis that closes a person and one that opens access, care, protection, contestation. Between a profile that owns you and a record you can correct. Everything depends on who owns the handle and which surface makes its use visible.

The cognitive default remains substantialist, however. This is the part that makes the matter less elegant. We can know perfectly well that something is a relation and still treat it as a substance. Hormuz shows this without requiring metaphysics: everyone knows its power depends on what passes through it, yet the name functions as a solid block. The same thing happens with “trusted user,” “risk,” “creator,” “talent,” “vulnerable family,” “problem student,” “borderline content,” “sacrifice zone.” No one has to believe in the essence. The machine only needs a stable point from which to decide.

The corridor does not need svabhāva to be true. Its habit is enough.

That is why purely dissolving criticism often arrives late. It says: do not substantialize. Meanwhile the system has already substantialized just enough to rank, grant access, reward, exclude, renew. Then, when you demand an account, it turns fluid again: complex model, many factors, dynamic policy, evolving process, no single cause, no single author. First it solidifies in order to act; then it dissolves in order not to answer. An old trick with a new interface. Stone when it strikes, fog when you need to grasp it.

Śūnyatā, left on its own, risks blessing the second half of the trick.

And yet throwing it away would be a mistake. Without its diagnosis, re-discretization becomes nostalgia for substance. It becomes rigid identity, pure community, sacred territory, true nature, original subject. The kind of stuff power knows perfectly well how to use; often it loves the stuff. The point is not to return to essences. It is to use anti-substantialism as the first half of the gesture, not as the whole gesture. First dissolve the idol. Then build a deliberate handle. Stop after the first move and you have cleared the table for somebody else.

The practical formula is almost embarrassing: emptiness does not mean no leverage.

A category can be empty of intrinsic nature and full of consequences. A procedure can be constructed and still wound. An identity can be historical and still protect. A boundary can be contingent and still keep everything from becoming feed. The corridor wins when it forces us to choose between false essences and administered fluidity. Resistance must reject both: no sacred nature, no dissolution without purchase.

This also holds for the stage voice. The synthetic actor, the artificial double, the gentle front stage, the ambient chorus of social signals: all can speak an anti-substantialist language. They can tell you there is no single author, that the system learns, that feedback is collective, that experience emerges, that trust is distributed. All plausible. All perhaps true. But the vulgar question remains: where is the diff? Who changed what? Where can I contest it? Who can roll it back? Which voice creates an obligation, and which leaves only warmth? Without that cold line, dissolving the author emancipates no one. It moves responsibility into a more elegant fog.

The previous chapter said that freedom is not passing once, but not having to become, each time, the version of yourself the renewal desk knows how to renew. The point tightens here: it is not even enough to know that version is constructed. The desk can grant you that. It can even celebrate it. It can give you a form for updating your profile, a “preferences” field, a fluid narrative of your identity, empathic assistance that believes in no essence. But if the criterion of validity remains its own, fluidity is only more frequent renewal.

Involuntary capture happens when a true criticism removes the old grip from the dominated without imposing a new one on the dominator.

That is why the book keeps returning to ugly objects: diffs, logs, rollback, stockpiles, strikes, public records, forums, nonpunitive withdrawal. They are not spiritual words. They promise no redemption. They are handles. And handles are what remains once you understand that no substance will save you and no proprietary flow will set you free out of sympathy.

The best śūnyatā for this book is not final dissolution. It is the warning against the false fullness of categories. It keeps re-discretization from becoming a new idol. But immediately afterward it must yield to construction: this category serves this purpose; this trace is contestable; this lever can stop this effect; this collective can speak here; this exit does not cost everything; this correction leaves a history.

Without that second half, emptiness becomes furniture in the corridor.

With that second half, perhaps it becomes discipline: do not believe in the handle’s intrinsic nature, but do not surrender it. Know that the object is constructed and, for precisely that reason, defend who may construct it, modify it, withdraw it, break it, remake it. Do not worship the discrete. Use it against those who prefer you continuous where you cannot bite and discrete only where they can bite you.

The corridor fears this form of intelligence because it cannot dismiss it as essentialism and cannot absorb it as fluidity. It is too constructed to be sacred, too rough to be user experience.

Perhaps illegible resistance is not simply becoming empty. Perhaps it means learning when to dissolve and when to crystallize. Empty before the idol, discrete before power. Without svabhāva, yes. Without a handle, no.

[^sunyata-nagarjuna]: In Madhyamaka terminology, the minimum reference is Nāgārjuna’s *Mūlamadhyamakakārikā*: the link among pratītyasamutpāda, śūnyatā, and the absence of svabhāva prevents emptiness from being treated as simple nihilism or as a new negative substance.
