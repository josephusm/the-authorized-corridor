# The Internal Gap

After the body that withdraws and the translator who remains, Part IV seems to have exhausted its moves. If you can remove yourself, vacancy. If you cannot withdraw without betraying someone, the tired ribosome: you remain, but try to make the cost of translation attributable. In between lie flat as ambiguous withdrawal, and re-discretization as the reconstruction of handles where the corridor had left only flow.

These are still postures toward the outside.

The internal gap begins when posture is no longer enough. When the subject cooperates, speaks, signs, translates, fills in the form, accepts the appeal process, makes themselves readable, submits the right document, uses the right language, follows the right format—and still something remains unresolved. Not because the subject cleverly conceals it. Not because they guard a private sanctuary where power politely declines to enter. That would be nice. It would also be false, so let us leave it to the spiritual pamphlets.

The internal gap is drier: the scanner has finite resolution.

The Hormuz dream said it almost comically, as dreams do when they cannot be bothered with elegance: the body disassembled into percentages, 47% contradictions, 23% surprise, 30% unadministered unease. The corridor did not kill the body. It reassembled it without the parts that did not fit the model. Outside, the hand of sand. The image holds because it does not describe censorship in the classical sense. It describes a sampling error elevated into an administrative ontology.

The scanner does not see people. It sees tractable components.

Contradiction, surprise, unease: these are not convenient components. A contradiction can become a red flag, a doubt can become risk, a delay can become anomalous behavior, a hesitant sentence can become low reliability. But something has already been lost in that passage. Internal grain is translated into an external event. Oscillation becomes signal. Signal becomes category. Category becomes treatment. Then someone arrives with a dashboard and says: we have understood the subject.

No. You have understood what was large enough to cast a shadow on the sensor.

This is where the gap is not eliminated but displaced. Every attempt to close the corridor produces another zone of contingency. The closed loop eliminates the occasion for verification, but it does not eliminate the need for verification; it degrades it. The disintegrated state does not eliminate politics; it moves it into more brutal negotiations. Software that erases the zone of human intervention does not eliminate judgment; it reconstitutes it as panic, workaround, emergency, personal blame. The gap is irreducible. Close it at the front and it reappears at the back. Close it outside and it reappears inside.

Here, inside does not mean intimate in the bourgeois sense. I am not talking about the soul's little bedroom, with the bed made and a quotation from Rilke on the nightstand. Inside means: at the limit of resolution between consciousness and format. The corridor can force you to produce more signal. It can increase frequency, granularity, depth, correlation. It can demand voice, face, heartbeat, logs, geolocation, history, relationships, signature, provenance, explanation. It can even ask you to narrate yourself, because self-narration is an open-pit mine for anyone who knows how to turn it into a profile.

But more signal is not more world.

Often it is the opposite. The more the system insists on sampling, the more it creates the illusion that everything that matters must appear as a sample. The part that does not sample well is not recognized as legitimate residue. It is read as noise, anomaly, incompleteness, bad faith, poor data quality. The subject is made responsible for the scanner's limit. A fine epistemic swindle: the sensor is nearsighted, and the object is blamed for failing to show up with enough contrast.

The internal gap, then, is not the subject's hiding place from the corridor. It is the point where the corridor mistakes its own resolution for the structure of reality.

This distinction keeps us from making two opposite mistakes. The first is mysticism: believing that there is a pure interior, inviolable in principle, where no machine will ever reach. Machines reach. Devices reach. Procedures reach. History is full of powers that learned to read what seemed illegible yesterday: accent, posture, social network, micro-expression, spending pattern, hesitation time before a button. Anyone who grounds freedom in what the system cannot yet read is merely signing a lease on the next scanner.

The second mistake is scanner determinism: believing that, given enough data, computation, and patience, consciousness becomes fully discretizable. That is metaphysics disguised as engineering. Not because consciousness is magical, but because the relation between experience and format is not a simple technical loss. Every format decides what can count as difference. Every act of sampling decides in advance which variations count as variations. Every category already carries a politics of detail.

The problem is not only that the scanner sees too little. It sees according to a grammar.

Caronia is useful here, but he must be used without being embalmed. If the digital produces a proliferation of floating signifiers, the corridor responds by reducing overflow: slots, categories, provenance, signature, standards, formats, review, classifier, policy. The system tries to turn semiotic excess into administrable flow. So far we are still within language. The internal gap pushes one step lower: there is a pre-formal grain that is not yet available as signification. It is not a signifier looking for an anchor. It is not a forbidden word. It is the micro-interval before the word, the change in pressure before it becomes a sentence, the contradiction before it chooses a readable mask.

The corridor wants to skip that point.

Not out of psychological malice. By architecture. The corridor cannot work with what has no edge. It has to establish an edge, a name, a class, a state, a responsible party, a timestamp, an appeal channel. Even when well-intentioned, even when it opens a real forum, it has to turn the grain into a case. The DSA, redress bodies, and review procedures are important counterexamples to the wall without guards: they produce records, impose costs, create a handle. Good. But they arrive after something has already been made into an object of decision. The forum can correct an error; it cannot always restore the point at which the person was not yet a case.

That does not make it useless. It makes it late.

A late appeal can recognize the wrong and leave the damage in place like furniture. A student accused by an opaque detector may be cleared weeks later, but trust has been stained in the meantime. Content may be restored, but its moment of peak attention is dead. A worker may win a dispute, but the platform's rhythm has already reshaped their choices. A person may obtain an explanation, but only after translating themselves into the kind of person entitled to ask for one. The corridor returns the repaired object; it does not always return the time in which that object was used against you.

The internal gap also inhabits this time.

It is the part of experience that does not coincide with the case file of harm. The fear before the sanction, the tiny calculation that makes you leave something unsaid, the preventive correction of your sentence, the way a possible category alters your voice before anyone applies it. The corridor loves documentable harms because they can become tickets. It hates the micro-shifts that precede the ticket. There is no event there, no decision, no formal error. Only a change in internal posture. Precisely: a grain.

And grain does not make good governance.

This is where the material pre-diff enters. It is not enough to look at the last screen, the last moderator, the last mediator, the last decision. Translation begins upstream: in rack costs, leases, credit, the region being served, the language worth supporting, the latency that makes an appeal practicable or turns it into bureaucratic furniture. We should not turn the balance sheet into a magical cause. That would only be another fetish, this time wearing a tie. But when a material constraint decides which categories can exist, which languages are maintained, which time frames are acceptable, which appeals cost too much, then the scanner has already chosen its resolution before meeting the subject.

The internal gap is not outside the infrastructure. It is its unresolved remainder.

This is why the synthetic actor and the frontline manager are not opposites. One places a perceptual face in front of the corridor; the other holds the social seam together behind it. Both translate. Both make livable a choice of resolution already made elsewhere. The synthetic actor makes the seam disappear because the scene appears to work. The local manager makes it disappear because the human relationship absorbs the friction. In both cases, the subject encounters a surface softer than the real carrier. The softness is not innocent. It is often how low resolution avoids appearing as violence.

The internal gap is what remains even when the surface works.

This changes how resistance should be understood. Vacancy withdraws substrate. Re-discretization reconstructs handles. The tired ribosome makes the cost of translation attributable. The internal gap does none of these things. It does not act. It does not make claims. It does not build. It does not interrupt. This is exactly why it must not be sold as a strategy. A strategy must be something that can be chosen, repeated, communicated, taught. The internal gap cannot be taught as a technique. It can be recognized as the limit of someone else's domain.

The difference is subtle, so let me put it badly and clearly: you cannot use the internal gap against the corridor as you would use a strike or a parallel register. But you can stop believing that the corridor has read you simply because it has produced a coherent version of you. You can treat that version as a low-resolution projection, not as your administered truth. You can reject the blackmail that says every unformatted residue must become confession, content, symptom, explanation, or missing data.

Is that little? Yes. The real world has an unpleasant habit of not delivering complete liberations in gift-wrapped packages.

But this is also where capture loses some of its prestige. The corridor lives not only by control, but by epistemic authority: the claim that it can say what you are more operationally than you can. If the subject accepts that claim completely, the profile is no longer merely a profile; it becomes delegated introspection. The system does not merely classify you. It teaches you to feel as classified. The internal gap interrupts this passage. It does not deny that classification has real effects. It denies that those effects exhaust the field of reality.

A part of you remains, not because it is pure, but because the format does not have enough pixels.

This has a modest but hard political consequence: every decent forum should be measured not only by whether it corrects the final error, but by whether it allows the resolution itself to be contested. Not only: this decision is wrong. Also: why does this category exist? Why is this latency acceptable? Why does this language arrive late? Why does the appeal cost more than the harm? Why can the mediator not be revoked? Why does contestation begin only after I have already become a case?

If these questions have no place, the forum is almost clerical: it hears confession within the grammar that has already decided what guilt can be.

A less indecent corridor would not promise to see everything. That promise is always a threat. It would expose its own limits of resolution, leave records that can be amended, accept the symmetrical cost of correction, allow category and framing to be contested before they become furniture, and make the entire seam visible: voice, database, interface, mediator, carrier, material constraint. Not because the gap would then disappear. Because at least it would not be sold as absence.

The internal gap closes Part IV by lowering the pitch of hope. Good. Hope pitched too high is often just a drone with warm lights. Vacancy, lying flat, the tired ribosome, and re-discretization mark points where the corridor can lose its grip. The internal gap says something colder: even when it does not lose its grip, it does not own everything it claims to have resolved. There is a difference between being treated and being known. Between being sampled and being exhausted. Between being converted into signal and coinciding with the signal.

That difference does not save anyone by itself. It does not pay the rent, cancel a sanction, reopen an account, protect a student from a stupid detector, stop a classifier, break a contract, or overthrow a carrier. It is a poorer threshold. But poor thresholds have one virtue: they do not lie about their strength.

The corridor can close many doors. It can make speech costly, withdrawal suspect, care invisible, appeal late. It can learn to read ever subtler signals. It can sell that reading as knowledge. The internal gap remains the point at which this sale should not be accepted. Not because an inviolable essence lives there. Because every scanner sooner or later mistakes its grid for the world, and that mistake is precisely where the ledger must be scratched by the hand of sand.

Letting the accounts fall onto the floor is not enough to win. But at least it shows that the floor was there.
