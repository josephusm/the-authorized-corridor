# Lying Flat and Illegibility

Vacancy describes the point at which the body stops giving the corridor the proper posture. Lying flat begins one step later, when that gesture no longer remains merely a body lowering itself but becomes a social form visible enough to be named and ambiguous enough that no one immediately knows what to do with it.

This is where illegibility becomes dangerous even for those who seek it.

As long as I am looking at a single horizontal body, I can ask which function it is ceasing to feed. When I look at a generation, a class, a community, a school, a trade being emptied out, the question changes: am I seeing withdrawal or resignation? A strike without a forum or distributed depression? A refusal of the carrier or simple desertification of the field? The same image sustains all these readings, and the corridor thrives precisely on this ambiguity. If it cannot use the gesture as an operational signal, it can use it as a diagnosis.

Diagnosis is the first capture of lying flat.

The system looks at the body lying down and says: motivational crisis, youthful fragility, low productivity, aspiration disorder, demographic risk, culture of disengagement. Or, from the more sympathetic but not always more useful side: burnout, self-care, anti-work, the right to slowness. These are different frames, some more decent than others. But they share a flaw: they translate illegibility into a legible object. Withdrawal becomes symptom. Symptom becomes policy. Policy becomes dashboard. And the body returns to verticality, at least as data.

It is not enough, then, to say that lying flat is resistance. That would be too convenient. The corridor loves formulas it can hang on a wall and use as a legend.

Lying flat is resistance only when it makes it harder for the device to produce its own proof of proper functioning. Not when a person is suffering. Not when a person gives up because they see no way out. Not when withdrawal is left alone, private, mute, ready to be explained by someone else. It becomes resistance when it interrupts a chain: the school that can no longer present engagement as learning, the platform that can no longer present activity as community, the labor market that can no longer present availability as consent, the institution that can no longer present unpaid repair work from below as functioning.

The point is proof. The corridor does not only need bodies to work. It needs bodies to prove that the world consuming them is still habitable.

A student filling out the questionnaire, a teacher working around yet another broken platform to save the lesson, a volunteer moderator holding together a community overrun by spam, a worker turning every failure into personal flexibility: all these gestures can be real care. I do not despise them. Without them, many things would fall on those most exposed. But the corridor also uses them as certificates. If someone holds the missing piece together, the system can say it is holding. If someone translates harm into the proper channel, the system can say it is listening. If someone remains vertical enough to deliver a signal, the machine can call that signal participation.

Lying flat breaks this certification only if it does not remain isolation.

This is where the wall without guards enters. The most effective threshold is not always the one with a turnstile. It is the one that no longer needs a turnstile because it has entered the bodies. No one forbids you to challenge; you are merely shown the proper place to do it. No one silences you; you are asked to open a ticket. No one denies your experience; you are asked to translate it into feedback. No one excludes the student, the parent, the teacher, the worker; the system simply decides which form of their speech will be treated as speech and which will be reduced to noise.

This is a harder threshold than prohibition, because it can present itself as openness.

School is a good stress test for precisely this reason. Not because it is a special case, but because it shows care, power, childhood, measurement, anxiety about efficiency, and the vocabulary of participation all at once. When AI or edtech enters with the promise of personalization, tutoring, detection, safety, workload reduction, the problem is not only whether the tool works. It is who can say that it does not work without being translated into the format of an individual complaint. Can the student correct the public double built from them? Can the parent challenge the classification without becoming “resistant to change”? Can the teacher refuse the promised efficiency without being read as backward, fragile, nostalgic, technophobic?

If the answer runs only through surveys, tickets, dashboards, advisory committees with no cost, or procedures that absorb speech as input, then the wall is already inside the room. No guard is needed. The body presents itself in the required posture on its own.

Lying flat, in this context, is not simply refusing to use the tool. That could be mere inability, fatigue, ignorance, privilege, or the inadvertent sabotage of those with fewer resources. Withdrawal matters when it stops giving the device the confirmation the device expected: login, use, adaptation, workaround, measurable enthusiasm, routed protest, silence interpretable as consent.

But the risk is immediate here too. If students stop speaking, the register will speak. If teachers stop correcting from below, the contract will speak. If a community closes, the old archive will speak. If a group refuses the channel, the risk category that the channel prepared for that refusal will speak. The corridor does not need the living when it owns enough cold residues to replace them.

This is why pure lying flat is politically unstable. It leaves a void, and every void inside an apparatus is filled by whoever already has the labels ready.

A minimal re-discretization is needed.

I do not mean an orderly return to the form. I mean rebuilding poor, local, actionable handles that cannot be fully digested. An off-format copy. A parallel, amendable register. A note with name, date, defect, effect, possibility of correction. A trace that does not ask to be recognized as beautiful participation but prevents the system from saying: there was nothing to read. The body remains less available, but does not surrender its silence to the enemy archive.

This is the difference between withdrawal and a strike without a forum.

The classic strike has a scene. It has a nameable adversary, a measurable loss, a constituency that can speak, a counterpart that can concede or repress. Lying flat often arises where that scene is missing. There is no negotiating table. There is no single master. There is not even always a formulated demand. There is a distributed system asking for posture, adaptation, and hope, and a mass of bodies discovering that they no longer have the desire or the margin to offer them.

Calling it a strike is tempting, but risks lying. Calling it apathy is worse, because it gives the corridor the explanation it wanted. Perhaps the point is drier: lying flat is negative pressure without a natural forum. It can become political only if it builds or finds a forum without ceasing to deny the system purchase.

Here the chimera returns. The corridor mixes centered and acentric layers. Against the centered layer, visibility is needed: a we, a name, pressure, a reputational or material cost. Against the acentric layer, the opposite is needed: circumnavigation, lowering the signal, local exit, refusing to feed metrics and classification. The isolated individual cannot do both. If they show themselves, they are processed. If they disappear, they are replaced. The constituency can, perhaps, become a chimera: visible enough to prevent diagnosis by others, illegible enough not to surrender its entire surface for reuse.

This is the part that breaks the fantasy of the horizontal hero. No body alone is a complete strategy. The body lying down can indicate a loss of grip, but it does not by itself build buffers, corrective archives, common language, a forum, mutual aid, a criterion of truth. Without these, lying flat slips into desertification. Not a moral defeat: a material defeat. The field empties out, and the corridor’s infrastructure remains, patient as mold.

Desertification is the second capture.

The first capture was diagnosis: I explain you away. The second is colder: I wait you out. If you do not participate, others will. If you do not speak, the old record will. If you do not work, the work will be offloaded elsewhere. If you do not moderate, the community will rot and then be closed for security reasons. If you do not teach beyond your contract, the platform will show that more automation is needed. If you do not correct the system from below, the system will use the deterioration as proof that more of the system’s tools are needed.

A neat trick, it has to be said. The corridor presents itself as the cause, then bills for the cure.

To get out of this circuit, lying flat must stop being merely a posture and become a selective threshold. I do not give everything. I do not disappear completely. I do not translate the harm into your language as free labor. But neither do I let you speak in my place using your residues. I take away my verticality, but leave a splinter precise enough to wound you if you misuse it.

The splinter is linguistic before it is technical.

Caronia helps here: when language, imagination, relationships, and affects become direct material for valorization, the gate does not only decide who enters. It decides which word remains language and which becomes algorithm, connection, engagement, procedure, noise. Lying flat that produces no word of its own is reduced to a pattern. Speech that immediately accepts the proper channel is reduced to input. Mockery, parody, expressive rebellion sting only if they open or protect a forum; otherwise they become digestible aesthetics, meme, campaign, teaching material for the carrier.

The minimal trace must therefore also be a defense of the word. Not a grand manifesto, not a full confession, not an identity profile to be processed. A small word that cannot be converted without visible loss: this is the defect, this is the date, this is the part I refuse to translate into your vocabulary, this is the person or group that can correct the trace, this is the cost if you pretend it is only feedback.

A poor, amendable, off-format copy. Not because poverty is sacred. Because the less surface it offers for reuse, the more it forces whoever touches it to show their hand.

Lying flat, then, is not the answer. It is a test. It shows whether the corridor needed a living subject or whether a residue was enough. It shows whether a community owns buffers of its own or was merely an unpaid department of the platform. It shows whether a school listens to words or collects signals. It shows whether a constituency exists or whether there were only exhausted individuals synchronized by the same pressure.

If withdrawal produces diagnosis and replacement, the corridor still has a grip. If it produces cost, common memory, and the possibility of correction outside the expected channel, the grip cracks. The system does not fall. The violins do not start. But for once the machine cannot call only compatible posture reality.

This is the most I can ask of lying flat without turning it into a religion.

No redemption. No purity. No universal strategy. A local loss of posture that, if surrounded by poor traces, buffers, and speech not reduced to input, can become useful illegibility. If left alone, it becomes private pain or manageable desert. If it speaks too much in the corridor’s language, it becomes a category. If it never speaks, it becomes a cold record.

The crack lies in between, as always. Ugly, unstable, with no marketing.

The next chapter must look at a different figure still. Not the body lowering itself, but the exhausted translator who keeps functioning: the tired ribosome. There the problem will no longer be removing posture from the corridor, but understanding when the substrate’s labor of translation stabilizes hybrid regimes that ought to fail. The question shifts: what happens when the body cannot lie down, because someone still depends on its ability to transform dead code into bearable life?
